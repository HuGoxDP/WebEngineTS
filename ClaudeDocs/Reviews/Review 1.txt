Overall Review Score, Comments, and Suggestions
1: good
This is a solid, technically competent paper with a well-motivated engineering contribution and a notably rigorous quantitative evaluation. The writing is clear, the related work is thorough, and the honesty about limitations is appreciated. The main weaknesses are: (1) the absence of VRAM measurement despite KTX2's primary benefit being VRAM reduction; (2) no integrated-graphics or mobile hardware tested, leaving the most constrained student devices uncharacterized; (3) the abstract's mismatched future tense; and (4) no user study to connect technical metrics to educational experience.
Suggestions for improvement:
1. Add VRAM measurement (e.g., via the WEBGL_debug_renderer_info extension or browser DevTools GPU memory reporting) to directly evidence the primary claimed benefit of KTX2.
2. Test at least one integrated-graphics configuration (e.g., Intel Iris Xe) to cover the most constrained educational hardware stratum.
3. Fix the abstract to use the past tense consistent with the completed evaluation.
4. Expand the deployment description; the paper mentions the engine is used in an actual educational platform, but provides minimal detail. Even brief information on the scale of deployment (number of students, scenarios deployed) would significantly strengthen the real-world impact claim.
5. Consider adding a small usability assessment, even an informal one, to bridge the gap between frame-time metrics and actual student experience.
